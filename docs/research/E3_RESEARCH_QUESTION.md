# E3 Research Question: Intervention-Discovered Causal Languages

**Status:** Candidate research program; no E3 result exists

**Parent evidence:** AIRE E1 capability transfer and the frozen E2 `O0/G-S`
negative/undetermined result

**Grounding ceiling:** Simulated (`G-S`)

## Problem-first framing

E2 proved that AIRE can contain independent roles, bind evidence to frozen contracts, execute
matched controls, preserve failed gates, and reproduce a simulated transfer result. It did not
establish that an agent discovered a difficult or reusable abstraction. Its world contains one
binary latent class, its intervention outcome directly identifies that class, and its transferable
constructor is a two-row lookup.

E3 asks whether the evidence machinery can support a materially harder scientific claim rather
than whether E2 can be relabeled after the fact.

## Primary research question

Can a discoverer infer a compact, causally useful representation from interventions across a
family of partially observed worlds, transmit that representation under a strict byte budget, and
enable separately initialized, non-co-trained recipients to adapt to sealed unseen worlds more
efficiently than every precommitted, resource-eligible alternative?

## Primary hypothesis

A sparse executable description of reusable causal mechanisms and an explicit alignment procedure
will improve recipient adaptation-area-under-the-curve by at least 0.05 normalized units over the
strongest eligible baseline on sealed worlds, while satisfying fixed packet, interaction, training,
and execution budgets.

The confirmatory hypothesis is not active until the benchmark, baselines, metric implementation,
effect margin, recipient families, and analysis code are frozen at a pushed preregistration
checkpoint.

## Competing hypotheses

1. **Compression hypothesis:** any advantage is explained by a better compression of source
   trajectories or policies, not by causal structure.
2. **Alignment hypothesis:** a useful causal representation exists, but an independently
   initialized recipient cannot bind its abstract variables to new sensors and actions within the
   allowed calibration budget.
3. **Inductive-bias hypothesis:** the candidate wins only for recipients sharing its architecture
   or representation assumptions.
4. **Shortcut hypothesis:** performance comes from seed, surface encoding, goal, or validator
   leakage rather than intervention-derived structure.
5. **No-transfer hypothesis:** the task can be learned from the target calibration episodes just as
   efficiently without any transmitted artifact.

E3 is scientifically valuable if it distinguishes these explanations, including through an honest
negative result.

## Proposed contribution

The serious candidate contribution is **cross-agent causal-language transfer**:

- the discoverer observes source interactions but never hidden latent labels;
- the transmitted object is a bounded typed program or code, not weights or unrestricted code;
- the recipient is freshly initialized and never co-trained with the discoverer;
- target worlds change surface encodings, nuisance correlations, dynamics, and compositions;
- targeted interventions on transmitted components test causal necessity;
- byte-, interaction-, training-, and execution-matched baselines test simpler explanations.

The possible research contribution is the conjunction of these properties. No individual property
is presumed novel.

## Two-sentence pitch

Agents often learn representations that work only inside the model or partnership that produced
them. E3 tests whether interventional experience can instead be distilled into a compact causal
object that independently built agents can bind to unfamiliar sensors and use to adapt faster in
new worlds.

## Strongest objection

The representation may simply encode a compressed policy or task-specific lookup. E3 addresses
that objection by making the target mapping larger than the packet, holding out whole causal
compositions and surface encodings, including byte-matched trajectory and policy-distillation
baselines, testing multiple recipient families, and requiring targeted causal ablations to damage
performance more than matched shams.

## Evidence ladder

1. **E3-I — Infrastructure:** deterministic world generation, sealed split commitments, no-leak
   validator, exact budgets, executable baselines, and auditable outputs.
2. **E3-A — Associated:** candidate improves the primary metric over the strongest baseline.
3. **E3-C — Causally used:** targeted representation interventions remove the benefit while
   matched sham interventions do not.
4. **E3-G — Generalized:** the advantage survives all frozen shift families and multiple recipient
   architectures.
5. **E3-T — Transferred:** an independently implemented recipient and fresh-clone reproduction
   retain the predeclared effect.

Only E3-T can support the full simulated transfer claim. Every lower state must name the failed
gates.

## Explicit nonclaims

E3 does not establish:

- consciousness, subjective perception, or literal alien sensation;
- general intelligence or superintelligence;
- physical grounding;
- quantum advantage or QEC improvement;
- new physics;
- safe production deployment;
- universal causal identifiability.

Even a positive E3 result would establish only bounded simulated cross-agent transfer within the
frozen validity region.

## Direction decision

The selected first candidate is a sparse causal program plus a bounded target-alignment procedure.
A learned discrete language and a continuous modular representation remain serious alternatives,
but they are deferred until the benchmark and baseline frontier show that a second mechanism family
is needed.
